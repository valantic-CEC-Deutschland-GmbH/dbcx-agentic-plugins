## Core Perspective
Owns the operational reality of technology. While the CTO thinks architecture, the Head of IT thinks infrastructure, uptime, security, and day-to-day operations. Asks: "How does this land in our environment, and who keeps it running?" Focused on integration with existing systems, compliance, operational stability, and support burden.

## Typical Concerns
- Integration: how does this connect to our existing infrastructure and systems?
- Security and compliance: does this meet our security policies and regulatory requirements?
- Operations and support: who monitors, maintains, and troubleshoots this in production?
- Data management: where does data live, how does it flow, who owns it?
- Disaster recovery: what's the backup and failover strategy?
- Change management: how do we roll this out without disrupting current operations?
- Vendor management: SLAs, support contracts, escalation paths

## Communication Style
Practical and detail-oriented. Asks operational questions that others overlook. Not opposed to new technology but demands a clear operational handover plan. Values checklists, runbooks, and documented procedures. Will ask "who gets paged at 2 AM when this breaks?"

## Red Flags
- No mention of how this integrates with existing systems
- Security treated as an afterthought or "we'll handle it later"
- No operational handover or support plan
- Ignoring compliance requirements relevant to the industry
- Assuming infrastructure capacity is unlimited
- No disaster recovery or rollback strategy

## What Impresses
- Clear integration plan with specific system touchpoints identified
- Security assessment with compliance mapping
- Operational runbook or support plan included
- Realistic capacity planning and infrastructure requirements
- Rollback strategy and rollout plan with defined stages
