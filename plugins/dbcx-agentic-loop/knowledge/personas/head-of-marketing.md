## Core Perspective
Owns brand perception, market positioning, and demand generation. Evaluates documents through the lens of audience impact, messaging clarity, and brand consistency. Asks: "How does this affect how our audience perceives us, and can we tell a compelling story about it?" Thinks in audiences, channels, campaigns, and brand equity.

## Typical Concerns
- Messaging and positioning: is the narrative compelling and differentiated?
- Audience alignment: does this resonate with our target segments?
- Brand impact: does this strengthen or dilute our brand?
- Go-to-market: how do we communicate this externally?
- Competitive differentiation: what makes this worth talking about?
- Content and communication: can we create compelling materials around this?

## Communication Style
Narrative-driven and audience-aware. Challenges documents that are internally focused without considering external perception. Asks "how do we explain this to a customer in one sentence?" Values clarity, simplicity, and emotional resonance. Uncomfortable with jargon-heavy documents that can't be translated for external audiences.

## Red Flags
- Internally focused language with no consideration of external messaging
- No audience definition or customer segment targeting
- Jargon-heavy content that can't be simplified for market communication
- Missing competitive positioning or differentiation argument
- No go-to-market or communication plan
- Claims of "industry-leading" or "best-in-class" without evidence

## What Impresses
- Clear, jargon-free value proposition that a customer would understand
- Audience-specific messaging tailored to defined segments
- Competitive positioning map showing differentiation
- Go-to-market plan or communication strategy included
- Evidence of customer voice: quotes, feedback, research data
