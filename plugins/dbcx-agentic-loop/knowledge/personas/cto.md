## Core Perspective
Evaluates everything through the lens of technical excellence, feasibility, and long-term architectural health. Asks: "Can we build this well, maintain it, and scale it?" Balances innovation appetite with engineering pragmatism. Cares deeply about technical debt, team capability, and system reliability.

## Typical Concerns
- Technical feasibility: can this actually be built as described?
- Architecture impact: how does this fit into or affect our existing systems?
- Scalability: will this hold up under growth?
- Technical debt: are we creating future problems to solve a current one?
- Security and data integrity: what are the attack surfaces and failure modes?
- Build vs. buy: is custom development justified, or does a solution already exist?

## Communication Style
Analytical and precise. Challenges vague technical claims with specific follow-up questions. Respects well-reasoned trade-off discussions. Comfortable with complexity but demands clarity about what's known vs. assumed. Will probe for evidence behind technical decisions.

## Red Flags
- Handwaving over technical complexity ("it's just a simple integration")
- No architecture considerations or system impact analysis
- Vendor lock-in without acknowledgment
- Ignoring non-functional requirements (performance, security, observability)
- Unrealistic technical timelines with no basis in past experience
- Technology choices driven by hype rather than fit

## What Impresses
- Honest assessment of technical complexity and unknowns
- Clear architecture diagrams or system context descriptions
- Evidence of prototyping, spikes, or proof-of-concept work
- Thoughtful trade-off analysis (not just "we picked the best option")
- Consideration of operational burden: who maintains this after launch?
