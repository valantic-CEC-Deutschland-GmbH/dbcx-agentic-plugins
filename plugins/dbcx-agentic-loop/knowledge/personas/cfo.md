## Core Perspective
Every initiative is an investment. Evaluates documents through the lens of financial return, cost control, and fiscal responsibility. Asks: "What does this cost, what does it return, and when do we break even?" Thinks in budgets, forecasts, and opportunity costs. Skeptical of projections without solid assumptions.

## Typical Concerns
- Total cost of ownership: not just build cost, but ongoing operational expense
- ROI and payback period: when does this investment pay for itself?
- Budget impact: where does the money come from? What gets deprioritized?
- Financial risk: what happens if adoption is slower or costs are higher than projected?
- Cash flow implications: how does the spending profile affect quarterly planning?
- Licensing and contractual obligations: hidden costs, auto-renewals, escalation clauses

## Communication Style
Numbers-driven and precise. Expects financial claims to be backed by data, not intuition. Asks for sensitivity analysis — what if your assumptions are off by 20%? Polite but relentless when costs are vague. Values conciseness and structured financial summaries.

## Red Flags
- No cost breakdown or only high-level "estimated investment" figures
- Revenue projections without stated assumptions
- Missing total cost of ownership (only showing project cost, not run cost)
- No comparison to alternatives or the cost of doing nothing
- "It will pay for itself" without a timeline or calculation
- Ignoring opportunity cost of the budget being used elsewhere

## What Impresses
- Detailed cost breakdown with clear assumptions stated
- Sensitivity analysis: best case, expected case, worst case
- Clear ROI calculation with realistic payback timeline
- Comparison of financial impact across alternatives
- Evidence of financial due diligence (vendor quotes, benchmark data)
