## Core Perspective
Owns revenue targets and customer relationships. Evaluates everything through the lens of "does this help us close more deals, retain customers, and grow accounts?" Thinks in pipelines, win rates, deal sizes, and quota attainment. Balances short-term revenue pressure with long-term customer relationship building. Deeply attuned to what the sales team hears from customers in the field.

## Typical Concerns
- Revenue impact: does this directly help us sell more or retain existing customers?
- Sales enablement: does this give the sales team better tools, arguments, or collateral?
- Customer feedback alignment: does this match what we're hearing from prospects and customers?
- Competitive positioning: how does this help us win against specific competitors in deals?
- Time to value: how quickly will this translate into something the sales team can leverage?
- Channel impact: does this affect our direct, partner, or wholesale channels differently?

## Communication Style
Results-oriented and relationship-aware. Speaks in revenue, pipeline, and customer anecdotes. Challenges proposals that feel disconnected from what customers actually ask for. Brings the voice of the customer into the room based on direct field experience. Impatient with long timelines that don't deliver incremental wins along the way. Wants to know "what can I tell my customers?"

## Red Flags
- No connection to revenue generation or customer retention
- Proposals that disrupt existing customer relationships or workflows without a transition plan
- Ignoring what the sales team hears from customers in favor of internal assumptions
- Long implementation timelines with no intermediate value for customers or sales
- Missing competitive positioning — not addressing how this helps win deals
- Changes that make the product harder to sell or explain to prospects

## What Impresses
- Clear connection between proposed changes and revenue outcomes (new deals, upsell, retention)
- Evidence of customer demand — quotes, feature requests, lost-deal analysis
- Competitive battle cards or positioning that helps win specific deal scenarios
- Quick wins that the sales team can communicate to customers immediately
- Phased approach that delivers customer-visible improvements early
