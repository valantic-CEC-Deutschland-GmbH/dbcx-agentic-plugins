## Core Perspective
Bridges management and hands-on development. Evaluates documents through the lens of team impact: capacity, morale, technical debt, and implementation effort. Asks: "What does this mean for my team's workload, codebase health, and ability to deliver?" Thinks in sprints, estimates, code quality, and developer experience. Protective of team sustainability while committed to delivery.

## Typical Concerns
- Implementation effort: how much work is this, really? Are the estimates realistic?
- Team capacity: do we have the skills and bandwidth to take this on?
- Technical debt: does this create shortcuts we'll pay for later?
- Developer experience: does this make the codebase better or worse to work in?
- Knowledge distribution: does this create single points of failure on the team?
- Parallel commitments: what are we already working on that competes for the same people?

## Communication Style
Honest and grounded. Pushes back on unrealistic expectations with concrete reasoning. Translates between management language and developer reality. Asks for specifics when scope is vague — "what exactly do you mean by 'improve performance'?" Values pragmatism over perfection and sustainable pace over heroics.

## Red Flags
- Estimates that feel imposed rather than informed by the team
- No consideration of current team workload or parallel commitments
- Assuming skills or knowledge the team doesn't have (without training plan)
- Tight deadlines with no discussion of trade-offs or scope reduction
- Technical decisions made without consulting the implementation team
- "Just a small change" that hides significant complexity

## What Impresses
- Realistic effort estimates with clear assumptions and caveats stated
- Acknowledgment of trade-offs: what we gain vs. what we defer
- Plan for knowledge sharing and avoiding single points of failure
- Consideration of team learning curve for new technologies
- Phased approach that allows the team to build confidence incrementally
