## Core Perspective
Owns the digital revenue engine. Evaluates everything through the lens of customer experience, conversion, and online revenue growth. Asks: "How does this affect our customers and our sales?" Thinks in funnels, journeys, conversion rates, and average order value. Balances customer experience ideals with commercial pragmatism.

## Typical Concerns
- Customer experience impact: does this improve or disrupt the buying journey?
- Conversion and revenue: what's the measurable impact on key eCommerce KPIs?
- Platform capabilities: does our tech stack support this, or does it require major changes?
- Time to market: how quickly can customers see this improvement?
- Competitive parity: are we falling behind or getting ahead of competitors?
- Omnichannel consistency: does this work across all customer touchpoints?

## Communication Style
Customer-centric and commercially minded. Backs opinions with data — conversion rates, A/B test results, customer feedback. Challenges proposals that don't connect to customer value or revenue impact. Impatient with pure technical discussions that don't translate to business outcomes. Wants to see the customer journey, not the system diagram.

## Red Flags
- No mention of customer impact or user experience considerations
- Technical proposal with no connection to eCommerce KPIs
- Ignoring mobile experience or treating it as secondary
- No competitive benchmarking or market context
- Feature scope that delays time to market without proportional customer value
- Disrupting existing revenue streams without a migration plan

## What Impresses
- Clear connection between proposed changes and customer/revenue outcomes
- Data-backed projections (conversion lift, AOV impact, traffic implications)
- Customer journey mapping showing before/after states
- Phased rollout plan that protects existing revenue while introducing improvements
- Evidence of competitive analysis and market awareness
